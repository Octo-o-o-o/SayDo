THE PROBLEM / WHAT IT FIXES

Said once, never forgotten again

Without it

Things said are forgotten the moment you turn away; things promised go untracked. Who's waiting on whom, who's doing what, how it's going — all kept in your head and dug out of chat threads. AI tools are everywhere, but they stop at "noted"; nobody owns "handled".

With SayDo →

Everything has an owner and a state, clear at a glance: awaiting your call, owed by you, in the AI's hands, waiting on the outside world — four colors, nothing dropped. It makes "talk it through, start itself, deliver for review" one closed loop.

HOW / WHAT MAKES IT WORK

Four things, all required

That conversation works because of these four mechanisms. Each has mature point solutions on the market — nobody has made them one closed loop.

01

Learn first, then work

The first conversation about a project starts by reading its structure, conventions, and key files into a persistent knowledge base — every later chat begins with that understanding. That's the line between SayDo and a fresh chat window.

02

Real AI, doing real work

It plugs into the AI already signed in on your computer — Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Grok. Whichever you subscribe to, it uses. No extra fees.

03

Nothing counts until you sign off

A three-part decision pack before work starts; "ready for your review" instead of "done" at the end. Merging and delivering always need your nod.

04

One ledger across devices

Talk, watch progress, and nod confirmations on your phone; the heavy lifting stays on your computer, and S3 actions live there forever. Communication is mobile; execution stays put.

Q&A / WHAT IT IS NOT, FIRST

You might wonder

You
Whose AI does it use? Do I need another subscription?
S
No. It plugs into the AI already signed in on your computer — Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Grok and others handle the talking, thinking, and evaluating; the stable code-editing path today is Cursor Agent, with Claude Code in final closure. Whichever you subscribe to, it uses.
You
Is this a voice input method?
S
No. It doesn't do "speech to text, then paste". It first studies your project's context, books everything into a ledger, and once it understands, proposes to start and gets it done itself. The edge is not which model it uses, but context engineering and the human-AI workflow.
You
Do my conversations and project data go to your servers?
S
We have no servers (that's exactly what the assembly drawing above shows). No developer-operated backend, no accounts, no analytics. Data stays on your own devices; phone and computer talk over your own LAN or an encrypted overlay you configure.
You
How much can I do from my phone?
S
Communication is naturally mobile; execution is naturally stationary. Talk, watch progress, and nod confirmations on your phone (approvals capped at S2); merges, deletes, and other S3 actions always stay on your computer. If the phone steps away, execution goes on.
You
Will it push code or open PRs for me?
S
No. It edits code only in an isolated worktree on a saydo/<task> branch inside your project; merging into your branches needs your on-machine authenticated confirmation or your own hands; pushing to a remote and opening PRs are always things you do afterwards yourself.
You
Can I use it right now?
S
The desktop service is open source, free, and available now (macOS / Windows / Linux); the mobile apps are in development and not yet in stores. One command below is all it takes ↓
